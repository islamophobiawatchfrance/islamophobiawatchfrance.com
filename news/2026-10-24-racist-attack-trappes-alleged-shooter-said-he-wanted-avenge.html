# Alleged racist shooter claims he targeted Black and Arab residents to avenge looted merchants

A 39-year-old man opened fire from a vehicle in Trappes and La Verrière in the Yvelines department on Friday, October 23, killing one person and wounding five others. The alleged shooter, identified as Kevin T., told police he wanted to avenge merchants whose stores were looted during protests on October 17 and that he deliberately targeted people based on their skin colour, according to reporting by Le Monde. The National Anti-Terrorism Prosecutor's Office has opened an investigation for "assassination in connection with a terrorist enterprise committed on grounds of true or supposed membership to a purported race."

What happened

Kevin T., born in 1987 and known to police but not to intelligence services, carried out what authorities are treating as a racially motivated mass shooting across two towns on Friday morning. Beginning around 10 a.m., he fired automatically from his car at multiple locations in Trappes, then continued to La Verrière before being stopped at a Kiloutou rental facility in Coignières, according to Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez and reporting by La Provence.

The deceased is Pengo Bossio Wangi, a 26-year-old MMA fighter from Poissy who was struck near square Louis Pergaud in Trappes, according to Le Figaro. Five others were wounded, including three in critical condition. Among the injured is a 12-year-old girl, Ramatou D., who sustained roughly a dozen gunshot wounds and was listed in "absolute emergency" status when emergency responders reached her, Le Figaro reports.

According to Mediapart, a copy of Mein Kampf was discovered at Kevin T.'s home. An immediate family member has been placed in custody by the National Anti-Terrorism Prosecutor's Office, La Provence reports. Police are investigating Kevin T.'s stated motivation: he told officers he wished to "avenge" merchants affected by looting on October 17 and that he targeted "Black people and Arabs," Le Monde reports.

Reaction

Ali Rabeh, mayor of Trappes, described the attack as "objectively an attack with racist connotations, probably Islamophobic," according to La Provence. "A 12-year-old girl is between life and death as we speak," Rabeh told the press. "Her crime, it is probably to be 'racialized.'" He called for "republican concord" and stressed collective responsibility to prevent further violence.

Sabrina Sebaihi, an ecologist member of parliament, stated: "The victims were targeted for their origin, real or supposed. Let us say the words: it is racist terrorism that has struck. They were shot at because of the colour of their skin," according to TSA Algerie. She connected the attack to patterns of political discourse designating neighbourhoods, faces, and names as "enemies within."

Approximately forty Muslim actors and leaders across France, many involved with the Forum of Islam in France, issued a collective statement through Saphirnews calling for "responsibility and protection for all" in the face of what they characterized as hatred and violence targeting their communities.

Why it matters

The shooting represents a moment of acute tension in France's ongoing debates about integration, immigration, and secularism. Muslim leaders and left-leaning politicians have seized on the attack as evidence of real-world consequences stemming from anti-immigration and Islamophobic rhetoric in French public discourse. The Parquet national antiterroriste's framing of the crime as terrorism motivated by racial animus signals official recognition of far-right extremist violence as a national security threat rather than isolated criminality. The attack also reopens questions about the adequacy of threat assessment when suspects are known to police but not flagged by intelligence services.

Background

Trappes is a commune of approximately 35,000 residents in the Yvelines department, part of the Île-de-France region outside Paris. It has historically housed significant immigrant and working-class populations. The attack occurred in the context of civil unrest following October 17 protests that saw merchants' stores looted. France's laïcité framework—a constitutional principle of strict separation between religion and state—has become increasingly contentious over debates about Muslim integration and religious expression in public spaces. The Parquet national antiterroriste (National Anti-Terrorism Prosecutor's Office) is France's central authority for investigating acts it deems terrorism-related, regardless of perpetrator ideology.
