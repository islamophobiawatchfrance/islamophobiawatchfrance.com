# Nanterre Prefecture Orders Ibn Badis Institute to Stop Teaching Minors in New Administrative Clash

The Hauts-de-Seine prefecture has banned the Ibn Badis Institute in Nanterre from hosting minors for extracurricular activities, effective Sunday, 22 November, following a departmental inspection. The institute has announced it will contest the order in administrative court while complying with the directive, claiming the prefecture is subjecting it to "administrative harassment."

What happened

A prefectural order issued by the Hauts-de-Seine prefecture on 22 November prohibits the Ibn Badis Institute from welcoming minors for extracurricular activities, immediately halting Arabic language courses and religious instruction for children at the facility. The ban followed a control inspection conducted that same week by the Departmental Service for Youth, Civic Engagement and Sports (SDJES).

The Ibn Badis Institute responded swiftly to the order. In a statement, the institute declared: "We will apply this order, as we are committed to respecting the law, but we will contest it with the utmost firmness." The institute announced it has filed an urgent referral with the administrative tribunal to challenge the prefecture's decision.

Dispute over classification

The core disagreement centres on how the prefecture classifies the institute's activities. According to the Ibn Badis Institute, the prefecture is treating the Arabic language and religious education courses as collective youth reception activities (ACM—accueils collectifs de mineurs) subject to formal declaration and regulatory oversight. The institute contests this characterization, arguing instead that "the activities in question are primarily educational and religious transmission activities, designed to allow children to learn their religion, discover their values and develop their [knowledge of their faith]."

Reaction

The Nanterre municipal authorities have questioned the prefecture's decision, with the city suggesting the administrative action represents "administrative harassment" directed at the Ibn Badis Institute. The city has not yet announced whether it will publicly support the institute's legal challenge.

Why it matters

This dispute reflects ongoing tensions in France over how municipal and prefectural authorities regulate religious education facilities, particularly those serving Muslim communities. The case raises questions about the distinction between religious instruction and youth welfare regulations, and whether such classifications disproportionately affect Muslim-led institutions. The outcome could influence how similar facilities nationwide are regulated under French administrative law.

Background

The Ibn Badis Institute is a cultural and educational association in Nanterre, a working-class suburb northwest of Paris in the Hauts-de-Seine department. France's prefectural system gives prefects—appointed state representatives—significant authority over local administrative matters, including licensing and regulation of youth activities. Extracurricular youth activities in France typically fall under ACM regulations, which mandate formal declaration, trained staff, and compliance with safety and welfare standards. Religious organisations offering education to minors sometimes dispute whether their activities qualify as ACM-regulated services or fall instead under religious instruction exemptions, creating recurring administrative conflicts. The prefecture's authority to issue such orders and the institut's right to challenge them through the administrative court (tribunal administratif) are established aspects of French administrative procedure.

Q&A

Who issued the ban and when did it take effect?
The Hauts-de-Seine prefecture issued the prefectural order on 22 November 2026, and it took effect immediately that same day, prohibiting the Ibn Badis Institute from hosting minors for extracurricular activities.
What specific activities are affected?
Arabic language courses and religious education classes for children offered by the Ibn Badis Institute as extracurricular activities have been halted under the order.
What is the Ibn Badis Institute's legal argument?
The institute argues that its activities constitute religious education and transmission of faith values, not collective youth reception activities (ACM), and therefore should not be subject to ACM regulations and formal declaration requirements that the prefecture is enforcing.
How has the municipal government responded?
The Nanterre municipal authorities have questioned the prefectural decision as potentially constituting "administrative harassment" directed at the Ibn Badis Institute, though the city has not formally committed to supporting the institute's legal challenge.
