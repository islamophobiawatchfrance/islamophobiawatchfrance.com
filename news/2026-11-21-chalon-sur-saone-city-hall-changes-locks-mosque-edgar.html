# City Hall Changes Locks at Chalon-sur-Saône Mosque Following Court Order

The municipality of Chalon-sur-Saône changed the locks of a mosque on Edgar-Quinet Street on Monday, 16 November, following a September court ruling that ordered the expulsion of the Muslim association occupying the building. Mayor Gilles Platret announced the action as the resolution of a dispute over a municipal property that has housed the mosque since 1979.

What happened

On the morning of 16 November, the city reclaimed possession of the former Sainte-Marie market hall, which Platret described as "induly occupied by a mosque since 1979." The mayor, elected as a centre-right independent, stated that "the 1905 law of separation is now therefore respected in Chalon." The lock change followed nearly two months after the administrative court's decision.

On 22 September, the administrative tribunal of Dijon issued an order granting the municipality the right to expel the association managing the mosque. According to Platret, the city had lent the publicly owned building to the Muslim association free of charge, which he contended violated principles of secular law.

Reaction

No statements from the Muslim association or community representatives are reported in available sources as of publication.

Why it matters

The case reflects ongoing tensions in France between municipal authorities and Muslim communities over religious space and the application of the 1905 law of separation between church and state. The dispute illustrates broader questions about how French municipalities interpret laïcité—the constitutional principle of state secularism—when managing public property and religious associations. The lock change marks an assertion by local government of control over municipal assets and adherence to what officials frame as secular legal requirements.

Background

The 1905 law of separation established the legal framework for relations between religious organisations and the French state. Under this law, public authorities are generally prohibited from financing or supporting religious activities, though they may provide space to religious associations under regulated conditions. The law forms a cornerstone of French laïcité, the distinctive French interpretation of secularism that emphasises the state's neutrality toward religion.

Chalon-sur-Saône is a city of roughly 44,000 residents in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region in eastern France. Mayor Gilles Platret has pursued stricter enforcement of secular principles regarding municipal property use.

Q&A

Who took the action to change the locks?
The municipality of Chalon-sur-Saône, acting under its mayor Gilles Platret, changed the locks on the morning of 16 November.
What legal authority permitted this?
An order issued on 22 September by the administrative tribunal of Dijon granted the city the right to expel the Muslim association occupying the building.
How long had the mosque occupied the space?
The mosque had occupied the former Sainte-Marie market hall on Edgar-Quinet Street since 1979.
What was the mayor's stated objection to the arrangement?
Mayor Platret contended that the city's provision of free use of the publicly owned building to a religious association violated the 1905 law of separation between church and state.
