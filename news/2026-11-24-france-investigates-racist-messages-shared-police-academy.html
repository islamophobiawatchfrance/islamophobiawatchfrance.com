French Interior Ministry investigates racist messages at national police academy in Cannes-Ecluse, with two officer cadets suspended and facing disciplinary proceedings after Le Parisien reported the exchange of racist caricatures and Nazi imagery among trainees.

What happened

French Interior Minister Laurent Nunez confirmed on Monday that an administrative investigation had been launched at the National Police Academy in Cannes-Ecluse following reports of racist messages circulated among officer cadets. According to Nunez, approximately 30 students were interviewed as part of the inquiry, which led to the identification of two officer cadets as primarily implicated in the incidents.

The investigation was initiated after Le Parisien reported that racist messages, caricatures, and photographs—including an image depicting a Nazi salute—had been exchanged in private conversations among trainees at the academy. Nunez stated that "the first has been suspended and his situation will be examined by the academic board. The second is subject to disciplinary proceedings."

The Interior Ministry escalated the matter to judicial authorities under Article 40 of France's Code of Criminal Procedure. The prosecutor's office in Fontainebleau opened a preliminary investigation, and the National Police Inspectorate (IGPN)—France's police oversight body—was notified of the case.

Reaction

Interior Minister Nunez issued a statement condemning the conduct in the "strongest possible terms." "I strongly condemn, in the strongest possible terms, the unacceptable remarks and conduct, which have no place, even less so in an institution responsible for training future officers of the national police," he said via X (formerly Twitter). The academy's management launched the administrative investigation immediately upon learning of the incidents, according to Nunez's account.

Why it matters

The incident highlights ongoing concerns about racism and extremist ideology within French law enforcement institutions. The National Police Academy serves as the primary training ground for France's future police officers, making standards of conduct and vetting procedures particularly significant for public trust in policing. The dual administrative and judicial response signals official recognition of the seriousness of the allegations and reflects broader French efforts to address racist conduct within state institutions.

Background

The National Police Academy (École Nationale de Police) in Cannes-Ecluse, located southeast of Paris, trains officer cadets for France's national police force. The IGPN, formally known as the Inspection Générale de la Police Nationale, functions as France's internal police accountability mechanism, investigating allegations of police misconduct. Article 40 of France's Code of Criminal Procedure allows officials to report potential criminal offenses to judicial authorities, triggering formal investigation procedures. France's secular legal framework (laïcité) emphasizes equal treatment regardless of religion or ethnic background, making discrimination cases subject to both administrative and criminal sanction.

Q&A

What specific racist content was reported to have circulated among the cadets?
According to Le Parisien's reporting cited by Interior Minister Nunez, the exchanged materials included racist messages, caricatures, photographs, and an image depicting a Nazi salute. The academy's management did not publicly detail the full scope of the racist material when confirming the investigation.
What are the potential consequences for the two identified officer cadets?
The first cadet has been suspended, with his status to be examined by the academic board. The second cadet is currently subject to disciplinary proceedings. Both may face additional consequences depending on outcomes of the ongoing judicial investigation and IGPN review.
Which authorities are investigating the incidents?
The academy's internal administration conducted the initial inquiry. The prosecutor's office in Fontainebleau opened a preliminary investigation, and the National Police Inspectorate (IGPN) was notified and is involved in the ongoing investigation.
When was the incident brought to public attention?
French newspaper Le Parisien first reported the racist messages and imagery exchanged among officer cadets. Interior Minister Nunez's public statement confirming the investigation was made on Monday, 24 November 2026.
