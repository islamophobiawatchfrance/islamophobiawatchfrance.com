# Foundation of Islam in France Loses Public Utility Status as Kamel Kabtane Demands State Accountability

The Foundation of Islam in France has had its public utility status withdrawn, according to reporting from La Croix, with Kamel Kabtane, a prominent Muslim figure, calling the decision "a missed opportunity" and demanding accountability from the State regarding 10 million euros previously promised to the organisation.

What happened

The Foundation of Islam in France, a major institutional body representing Muslim interests in the country, has lost its public utility designation. This administrative status, which carries significant symbolic and practical weight in France, typically enables organisations to receive tax-deductible donations and carry greater legitimacy in civic affairs.

Kamel Kabtane has characterised the withdrawal as representing a missed opportunity for dialogue between the French state and Muslim institutions. His public criticism suggests disagreement with the decision-making process or the underlying rationale for the status removal.

The timing of the withdrawal raises questions about a separate commitment: the State had previously pledged 10 million euros to the Foundation of Islam in France. Kabtane's demand for accountability indicates uncertainty or concern about whether this financial commitment will be honoured following the loss of public utility status.

Reaction

Kamel Kabtane, speaking to Lyon Capitale, described the loss of public utility status as "a missed opportunity," signalling that Muslim community representatives view the decision as counterproductive to state-Muslim relations. His simultaneous call for State accountability over the promised 10 million euros, reported by Lyon Mag, suggests he sees a disconnect between official commitments and administrative actions.

The framing of the withdrawal as a "missed opportunity" indicates that institutional Muslim leadership had expected—or hoped for—a different outcome, one that would have preserved or strengthened the Foundation's formal standing within French civil society.

Why it matters

The Foundation of Islam in France operates as one of the primary institutional bridges between French Muslim communities and state structures. The withdrawal of public utility status represents a significant administrative setback for Muslim representation in a country where the relationship between Islam and laïcité (France's secular model separating religion from state affairs) remains contentious. The unresolved status of promised state funding adds financial uncertainty to institutional Muslim capacity-building at a moment when French policymakers are intensely focused on integration and security concerns affecting Muslim populations. Kabtane's vocal response signals that Muslim leadership will not accept such decisions without demanding transparency and justification from government authorities.

Background

The Foundation of Islam in France was established to serve as an institutional representative body for French Muslims and to facilitate dialogue between Muslim communities and state officials. Public utility status in France is a formal administrative designation granted to organisations deemed to serve the public interest; it allows these organisations to receive tax-deductible donations and carries prestige in civic engagement.

Kamel Kabtane is a prominent Muslim religious and community figure in France. The 10 million euro commitment from the State appears to have been part of a broader initiative to support Muslim institutional development and integration. Public utility status is particularly valuable in the French context, where laïcité—the constitutional principle of strict separation between religious institutions and state functions—shapes how religious organisations operate. The withdrawal of this status thus carries both practical and symbolic implications for how the State relates to organised Muslim institutional life.

Q&A

Why would the Foundation of Islam in France have had public utility status in the first place?
Public utility status is typically granted by French authorities to organisations serving demonstrable public benefit, including educational, charitable, and civic functions. The Foundation of Islam in France apparently met those criteria when the status was originally granted, suggesting it was recognised as contributing to social integration and community welfare.
What does loss of public utility status mean for donations?
Organisations with public utility status can receive tax-deductible donations from individuals and corporations. Losing this designation eliminates that tax incentive, potentially reducing fundraising capacity and limiting the organisation's ability to expand programmes or operations.
Is there a political dimension to this decision?
The sources do not provide details about the reasoning behind the status withdrawal or identify which government body made the decision. Kabtane's framing as a "missed opportunity" and his demand for accountability suggest he views the decision as potentially avoidable or contestable, but explicit political motivations are not detailed in available reporting.
What is the significance of the promised 10 million euros?
The State's earlier pledge of this funding suggests a commitment to supporting Muslim institutional development. Its status following the public utility withdrawal remains unclear, which is why Kabtane has demanded State accountability on the matter.
