# Great Mosque of Paris marks centenary as landmark of French religious integration

The Great Mosque of Paris, one of France's most prominent Islamic institutions, marked its hundredth anniversary this year, celebrating a century of presence within the French Republic. The milestone reflects the mosque's long-standing role in French Muslim life and its institutional relationship with the French state, according to reporting from RFI.

What happened

The Great Mosque of Paris, located in the 5th arrondissement, reached its centenary in 2026, marking one hundred years since its official opening in 1926. The anniversary represents a symbolic moment for the institution, which has served as the spiritual and administrative centre for many French Muslims and has historically maintained formal ties to the French state through various institutional arrangements.

The mosque's existence across this century underscores a particular model of Muslim integration within France's secular framework. Unlike many European Muslim communities that emerged primarily through post-war migration, the Great Mosque of Paris was established earlier, with roots extending to French colonial connections in North Africa and the Middle East.

Reaction

The anniversary has been framed by mosque officials and supporters as evidence of Islam's legitimate place within French civic life. The centenary messaging emphasizes the institution's continuity and its role in what might be described as a historically rooted French Muslim presence, distinct from more recent immigration narratives that sometimes dominate contemporary debates.

The milestone occurs amid ongoing tensions in France surrounding Islam, secularism, and the boundaries of religious expression in public life—debates that have intensified over the past two decades through disputes over headscarves, halal slaughter, mosque funding, and representations of Muslims in media and politics.

Why it matters

The Great Mosque's centenary narrative carries significance in contemporary France as a counterpoint to framings of Islam as a recent or foreign presence. For French Muslims, the institution's longevity can represent historical legitimacy and integration. For broader French society, the mosque's century-long presence raises questions about how France manages religious diversity within its republican secular model, and how historical institutions are recognized or forgotten in national memory.

Background

The Great Mosque of Paris was established during the 1920s, partly as a gesture toward France's North African colonial subjects and partly to serve Muslim communities already present in Paris. The French concept of laïcité—secularism—shapes how the state relates to all religious institutions, including mosques. France maintains a principle of state neutrality toward religion while funding secular aspects of religious buildings as cultural or historical monuments. The Great Mosque has historically navigated this complex relationship, maintaining religious functions while also serving as a cultural institution recognized by French authorities.
